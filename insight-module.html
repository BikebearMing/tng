Global Disputes Forecast

July 2, 2026

Geopolitical pressures, expanding disputes risk and cross-border complexities: Are organizations ready for the year ahead?

Download report

Executive summary

Geopolitics and trade policy are defining the disputes landscape in the year ahead. 79% of respondents identified tariffs, sanctions and export controls as external market factors increasing their organization's disputes exposure over the next 12 months.

This geopolitical reality is reshaping investigation concerns too: a staggering 82% of respondents fear being subject to a cross-border or multi-agency investigation in 2026, and more than half rank sanctions among their top investigation threats.

But alongside geopolitical risks, persistent threats remain. Respondents tell us cybersecurity, tax, employment and environmental, social and governance (ESG) concerns are also front of mind.

Managing these competing pressures is a delicate balancing act, and one that's hampered by constraints on resources. A notable 38% of respondents say their 2026 disputes budget is insufficient to meet current disputes risk, with limitations to funding and resourcing emerging as the top barrier to litigation preparedness.

To bridge the gap between disputes awareness and readiness, organizations need to evolve their risk frameworks and advocate for investment in disputes preparedness.

About the survey

The annual Global Disputes Forecast surveys 600 senior decision-makers with responsibility for, or who have a key role in, litigation at large organizations (annual revenue greater than USD 500 million) to understand the disputes landscape in the year ahead.

Key findings

  1. 01

    Technology, geopolitics and supply chain disruption drive external disputes risk

    As organizations face an external risk environment that is increasingly complex and uncertain, technology and data risks, geopolitics and trade policy, and operational and supply chain disruption are the top three external market factors increasing exposure to disputes

  2. 02

    Building resilience across multiple fronts becomes critical

    While cybersecurity and tax rank top of the types of disputes and investigations organizations expect to face in 2026, their risk profiles are also expanding across multiple categories, including trade/sanctions and ESG.

  3. 03

    Resource constraints expose vulnerabilities in organizations' risk-readiness

    38% of organizations report that their 2026 disputes budget is insufficient to meet current risks. Funding and resource constraints have also emerged as organizations' greatest barrier to litigation preparedness.
